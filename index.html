⭐ Featured • 3rd Place Award

MediGuard 2.0 — Smart, Secure Medicine Box with App Monitoring

An ESP32-based smart medicine box that gives voice and LCD alerts at scheduled times, verifies the patient by fingerprint or the caregiver by RFID, dispenses the correct compartment with a servo-driven rotating tray, confirms pickup with an IR sensor and reports dose status to a Blynk mobile app.

  • Controller & Scheduling: ESP32 DevKitC with built-in Wi-Fi and DS3231 RTC for up to 4 daily slots in EEPROM.
  • Dual Authentication: R307 optical fingerprint scanner (patient) and EM-18 RFID reader (caregiver) with 4×4 keypad enrollment (up to 9 users).
  • Servo Dispensing & Sensor: MG996R servo-driven rotating tray indexes compartment to outlet; IR sensor verifies physical pill pickup.
  • Audio & Cloud: DFPlayer Mini, PAM8403 amplifier and 3W speaker for voice prompts; Blynk IoT app for remote scheduling & refill mode.
  • Power Management: 2S2P Li-ion battery pack with BMS, Type-C charging, and LM2596 buck converter for uninterrupted power backup.
  • Recognition: Secured 3rd Place in Think & Thrive Technical Paper Presentation (SIMAT, March 2026).

⭐ Featured • IEEE Xplore Published

MediGuard 7X — Smart, Secure Medicine Box for Timely Medication

An Arduino Mega-based, fingerprint-secured medicine box with 7 time-slot compartments, LED and buzzer reminders, an automatic servo-driven lid and a solenoid lock — presented at IEEE ICCCMLA 2025 and published in IEEE Xplore.

  • 7 Compartments & LED Guidance: One per daily time slot; at scheduled intervals, buzzer alerts and active compartment lights green while others illuminate red.
  • Biometric Access & Locking: Patient unlocks via R307 fingerprint sensor; relay releases 12V solenoid lock, and MG996R servo opens the lid with live countdown on 16×2 LCD.
  • Power Distribution: 12V 5A power supply split through dedicated LM2596S buck converters into 9V, 6.2V, and 5V isolated rails for rock-solid stability.
  • Publication & Honors: Published on IEEE Xplore (DOI: 10.1109/ICCCMLA66092.2025.11581176); won First Best Idea Award at ICST 2025.

ICICS-2026 • VLSI Conference Paper Presentation

FinFET vs CMOS For Future Low Power Spintronics Devices And Circuits

Conference: 8th International Conference on Intelligent Circuits and Systems (ICICS-2026)
Dates: 21â€“22 August 2026
Organised by: School of Electronics and Electrical Engineering, Lovely Professional University (LPU), Phagwara, Punjab, India
Theme: SDG 4: Quality Education
Authors: Arjun Raj A, Noel Jose Paul (Department of ECE, Lovely Professional University)
Status: Presented • Certificate No. 514975 (Issued 05-11-2026)

A comparative study of conventional planar CMOS and FinFET technology for low-power VLSI design. The paper explains why planar CMOS struggles at advanced nodes (short-channel effects, drain-induced barrier lowering and rising leakage), and how the FinFETâ€™s three-dimensional, multi-gate fin structure restores electrostatic control of the channel. Both technologies are compared on leakage power, dynamic power, propagation delay, area and energy efficiency (power-delay product), drawing on results reported in existing research. The study traces FinFET adoption from the 16 nm to the 3 nm-class nodes, discusses its limitations (fin quantization, parasitic capacitance, process variability and fabrication complexity) and looks ahead to gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors. It concludes that FinFET is better suited to advanced low-power designs, but that the choice of technology should rest on a complete power-performance-area analysis rather than leakage reduction alone.

Key Research Highlights

  • Analysed short-channel effects, DIBL, and leakage limits of planar CMOS at scaled nodes.
  • Compared CMOS vs FinFET on leakage power, dynamic power, delay, area, and power-delay product (PDP).
  • Reviewed FinFET scaling trajectory across 16 nm, 10 nm, 7 nm, 5 nm, and 3 nm-class technologies.
  • Discussed FinFET physical challenges: fin quantization, parasitics, variability, and manufacturing complexity.
  • Identified future device direction: technology-circuit co-optimisation and gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheets.
